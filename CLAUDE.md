# BOULD

A browser side-scroller called BOULD (the name the owner's 4-year-old picked): you're a boulder, squish people, avoid spikes. Plain HTML/Canvas/JS, no build step, no dependencies. The owner "vibe codes" this from phone and desktop, so keep changes small, readable and easy to test.

## Layout
- `index.html`: page, canvas, on-screen touch buttons
- `game.js`: all game code (tunables at the top in `TUNE`, levels in the `LEVELS` array, then input, sound, update, draw, main loop)
- `PLAN.md`: concept, done list, ideas backlog. Update it when features land.
- Hosting: GitHub Pages deploys the repo root from the `main` branch (no workflow file needed)
- Workflow: one-developer project. No pull requests or feature branches needed: commit and push straight to `main` (this is the owner's standing instruction).

## Conventions
- Logical resolution is 320x180 in landscape. In portrait on a touch device `layout()` makes `W` about 195 and `H` as tall as fits above the buttons (whole-number CSS scale); the world is drawn bottom-aligned, shifted down by `topPad`, with extra sky above it. Use `WORLD_H` (180) for world-space heights (never `H`), and use `W` rather than 320 for anything screen-width related. The boss camera follows you inside the arena in portrait because the arena doesn't fit on screen. Test both: Playwright with `isMobile: true, hasTouch: true` at 390x844 and 844x390. Original note: CSS scales it with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Draw with integer coordinates and the `px()` / `disc()` helpers.
- All art is drawn in code. No image files unless the owner asks for them.
- Keep it a single `game.js` until it gets painful; then split into plain `<script>` files (no bundler).
- Game feel numbers live in `TUNE`; level layout lives in the `LEVELS` array (`LEVEL` is the current one). Prefer editing those over scattering magic numbers.
- Adding a level: append an object to `LEVELS` (name, theme `'day'`/`'night'`/`'castle'`/`'brain'`, `kind` of person `'person'`/`'zombie'`/`'knight'`/`'brain'`, hint, width, solids, and any of spikes / ghosts / lasers, people, checkpoints, goalX). `kind` picks how people look and act: people flee, zombies shamble toward you, knights march and raise their sword when you're close, little brains flee like people. A new theme needs a background function, a `SOLID_STYLE` entry, a pit style in `drawPits` and (for scenery) a `DECOR_KINDS` entry; a new kind of person needs a draw function in `DRAW_PERSON`, a `SQUISH_COLORS` entry and behavior in `updatePeople`.
- Ghosts are `[centerX, centerY, patrolRange, phase]`. They patrol back and forth, and when you come within `ghostChaseRadius` (6 boulder lengths) they drift toward you sideways only, never farther than `ghostLeash` past their patrol. They kill on touch like spikes. Placement rules: ground ghosts at centerY ~136 (you jump over them); never put a ghost's reach (patrol + leash) under a raised platform, since the low gap is a tunnel you can't jump in; keep their patrol 50px+ from pit edges; keep every checkpoint 40px+ from any ghost's reach.
- Lasers (level 3): `['gate', x, phase]` blinks floor-to-ceiling (off, 30-frame yellow warning, then deadly for 90 frames) and can't be jumped, so you wait for it to turn off; `['sweep', x, width, phase]` is a beam bobbing slowly between y~90 and y~144, so you go under when it's high or hop over when it's low. Keep lasers 80px+ from pits, keep checkpoints 60px+ from lasers, keep lasers out from under platforms, and leave a safe gap of 120px+ between two gates. People never walk through lasers (`walkLimits`). Use `phase` to offset timing between neighbors.
- Braintopia (level 4, theme/kind `'brain'`): synapses live in the `lasers` list as `['synapse', x, width, phase]`: two neurons on the ground with an arc between them that blinks like a gate (same timing, `gateState`) but only reaches ankle height (y 132 and down, hugging the bolt), so you hop it (keep width <= 44; 28-36 is comfortable) or wait for it to turn off. Same placement rules as lasers (80px+ from pits, checkpoints 60px+ away, nothing above them to bonk your head on); `walkLimits` already keeps people off them.
- Watertopia (level 5, theme `'water'`, kind `'monster'`): vents live in the `lasers` list as `['vent', x, phase]`. They blink like a gate (`gateState`, same timing) but the deadly column only reaches y `VENT_TOP` (66) down, far too tall to hop, so you wait for it to turn off. Same placement rules as gates (80px+ from pits, checkpoints 60px+ away, 120px+ between vents, nothing above them); `walkLimits` keeps monsters off them. No boss: it ends at `goalX`. It's "underwater": `LEVEL.tune` overrides `TUNE` values for the boulder's movement (gravity, fallGravity for falling only, maxFall, jump, maxSpeed, accel, airAccel, friction), so it's slower and floatier (jump apex ~60px instead of ~50). Any level can use `tune`.
- Big Brain (level 4 boss): `boss.type: 'brain'`. Same arena shape and ledges as the Giant Knight (copy those numbers), and you land on its top the same way (`hitW` = how wide its top is, `tip` = the intro hint, `colors` = particle colors). It floats toward you, charges up (`brainWindup`), shows dotted warning columns that follow you and then lock on for the last `brainLock` frames, then zaps floor-to-ceiling columns (`brainZap` frames), which hit you on the ledges too. Each hit adds a column (max 3). A boss in a level without `underground` just wins the level when defeated (no key/ending); `bossWall()` derives the portcullis from `arenaLeft`.
- Boss (level 3, `LEVEL.boss`): `{ name, x, hp, arenaLeft, camX, zoneMin, zoneMax }`. The Giant Knight is 64px tall, so his head (the only thing that hurts him) is out of reach of a jump from the ground (apex ~52px); you have to leap off one of the two ledges in the arena (tops at y=112) and land on his head from above. He walks between `zoneMin`/`zoneMax` (the gap between the ledges), telegraphs with a raised sword (`bossWindup` frames), then sweeps a low sword that only reaches ground level, so ledges and jumps over the sword are safe. Crossing `arenaLeft` starts the fight: a portcullis drops (`BOSS_WALL`) and the camera locks to `camX`. His HP persists across your deaths but the fight restarts at the checkpoint. A level with a boss has no `goalX`: beating the boss wins the level. Ledge edges must be 36px+ from the arena wall (for a run-up), and the ledges must be within ~85px of where he stands when chasing you, or they can't be leapt from.
- Smash (a core move, meant for reuse): jump, then press DOWN (ArrowDown / S / the ▼ button) to slam straight down. `TUNE.smashSpeed` sets the speed; it cancels sideways movement, shakes the screen on landing, and ends when you land. A solid with a 5th entry `'sand'` (e.g. `[x, y, w, h, 'sand']`) is smashable: normally solid, but a smash from above removes the whole piece (`smashSolid`, with sand particles). To make other smashable things (crates, ice, cracked floor), add a new type string next to `'sand'` in `updateBoulder`'s collision loop and a draw branch in `drawSolid`. Smashable pieces live in the runtime `solids` copy (rebuilt on each `resetRun`), so `LEVEL.solids` stays untouched; always use `solids` (not `LEVEL.solids`) for collision and drawing, except `drawPits`/`genDecor`, which use the original.
- Level 3 ending (after the boss): beating the Giant Knight drops a key (`key`, `hasKey`). The arena floor holds a smashable sand patch, a locked `'brick'` floor and a chamber with a `'cage'` solid and the baby rock (`baby`), plus a tunnel out. Landing on the brick floor with the key starts `ending` (scripted: key flies to the lock, floor slides open, you drop in, the cage unlocks, `ENDING_LINES` dialogue, both roll out through the tunnel, then the level is won). During `ending` the boulder follows `ending.ctrl` instead of your input, `die()` does nothing, and a tap or jump skips dialogue ahead. `LEVEL.underground` makes the camera scroll down when you're below the surface (`camY`, `maxCamY`); the world is drawn inside a `ctx.translate` layer, HUD and speech bubbles outside it. Tunnel floors must extend past the screen edge or the characters fall off the end of the world.
- Platforms you must jump onto should have a top at y >= 108 (jump height is ~50px).
- Level design rules learned the hard way: checkpoints must be on solid ground (not over a pit); don't put a platform within ~50px above the ground where a spike jump happens (the boulder bonks its head); don't pack a spike right before a pit.
- Spikes on a floating platform only make sense if the platform is long enough to jump over them (roughly spike width + 60px of platform on each side). Otherwise leave the platform spike-free.
- People never walk onto spikes or through lasers, and never past `goalX` (the final flag): `walkLimits` trims their walk range (and `resetRun` clamps their start), so no extra work is needed when adding them. Rule: no squishable enemy may ever be beyond the final flag, since touching it ends the level.
- Level 3 arena is the last piece of the level (width 3400); a level with a boss has no `goalX`: its ending script calls `state = 'win'`.
- Pits are drawn automatically as the gaps between ground pieces (`drawPits`: dark void, glowing bottom, mist). Keep pits <= 60px wide.
- Zooming is blocked on purpose (iPad Safari ignores `user-scalable=no`): the `// No zooming` block in `game.js` cancels pinch gestures, double-tap and trackpad pinch, and the CSS sets `touch-action: none`. Keep new touch handling compatible with that (use pointer events; don't rely on `click` or double-tap).
- Sound: all synthesized with Web Audio (no sound files). Helpers `beep` / `tone` / `noise` and the `sfx` table are at the top of the Sound section. Lasers, synapses and vents charge up when they enter their 30-frame warning and fire when they turn deadly (`HAZARD_SFX`, triggered from `updateHazardSound`, quieter the farther away you are); sweep beams hum, with the pitch following the beam. The Big Brain has its own `sfx.brainCharge` (1.25s, matches `TUNE.brainWindup`) and `sfx.brainZap`. Music is a small step sequencer (`MUSIC`: `calm` for levels, `boss` while a boss fight is active); add a track there and pick it in `musicTick`. Notes are MIDI numbers (60 = middle C), 0 = rest. M or the SOUND label mutes (remembered in localStorage).
- Kid-friendly: squishing is cartoony (stars and confetti, no gore).

## Running and testing
- `python3 -m http.server 8000` then open http://localhost:8000
- Jumping to a level: `index.html?level=2`; on the title screen pressing 1, 2, ... works; or click/tap the "LEVEL n" label at the top of the screen 3 times quickly to open a level picker (pauses the game; works mid-game; Esc or tapping outside closes it). The picker lists every entry in `LEVELS` automatically.
- `window.__game` exposes state for automated tests (Playwright is available in cloud sessions).
- After a change, check the browser console for errors and play through the part you changed.
